# ADR-2026-012: ChurchCore Is the ChMS Integration Target

**Date:** 2026-09-26
**Status:** PROPOSED (owner direction 2026-09-26; ratified with COUNCIL-2026-038)
**Deciders:** Ricardo Julia (project owner)
**Tags:** integration, chms, churchcore, product-family, identity

## Context

The 2026-09-26 MVP and competitive status report (`docs/reviews/2026-09-26-mvp-competitive-status.md`) lists "ChMS integration (e.g. Planning Center)" as a gap. Churches keep their people, households, groups and ministries in a church management system (ChMS). Without a sync, admins retype people into the LMS, and completions never flow back to the pastor's view of a member.

ChurchCore LMS belongs to a product family. **ChurchCore** (`github.com/ricardojjulia/ChurchCore`) is the family's ChMS: members, families, church memberships and roles, groups, ministries, ministry tracks, onboarding templates, events and attendance. It also has an embryonic `education_courses` / `education_enrollments` pair that duplicates what the LMS does properly.

## Decision

1. **The LMS's ChMS integration is with ChurchCore**, not a third-party ChMS. The competitive gap is closed by "ChurchCore + ChurchCore LMS work as one system." Planning Center, Breeze and others are reached indirectly: ChurchCore already imports from them (ChurchCore gap 4), so a church migrating to ChurchCore gets its people into the LMS as well.
2. **System of record:**
   - ChurchCore owns people, households, church roles, groups, ministries and onboarding.
   - The LMS owns courses, enrollments, progress, grades and certificates.
   - Each side sends the other only what it owns.
3. **ChurchCore's `education_courses` / `education_enrollments` are superseded.** When a church connects the LMS, ChurchCore shows LMS courses and completions (read-only, synced) instead. ChurchCore retires those tables through its own council process.
4. **Transport reuses the LMS's existing signed-delivery protocol.** The protocol has these properties:
   - Ed25519 signatures with `x-churchcore-key-id`, delivery IDs, timestamps and a staleness window (`src/lib/oneroster/signature.ts`).
   - Per-connection public keys.
   - Staging and preview before apply.
   - Identity linking.

   There's no new cryptography and no shared database.
5. **The two products keep separate databases and Supabase projects.** They connect per tenant (one ChurchCore church ↔ one LMS organization), never through direct database access.
6. **Sign-in:** members use their ChurchCore account to sign into the LMS through OpenID Connect (COUNCIL-2026-037). Identity links are keyed on stable ChurchCore IDs, never on email alone.

## Consequences

- The integration needs work in both repositories. The ChurchCore side (sender, OIDC provider, retiring the education tables) follows ChurchCore's own council process and signed-commit rules.
- Third-party ChMS connectors are out of scope unless a customer requires one. If one is needed, it must go through ChurchCore, not directly into the LMS.
- The status report's competitive table replaces "ChMS integration (e.g. Planning Center)" with "ChurchCore ChMS integration."
